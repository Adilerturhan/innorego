# Question 2

Based on the facts of a fictional case, answer the questions below. Your answers should demonstrate your knowledge of the relevant EU legal frameworks and concepts we discussed in the module on EU regulation of health data sharing.

Zanopi is one of the largest pharmaceutical manufacturers in Europe, currently working on a novel treatment for mood stabilisation and emotional wellbeing, specifically to provide personalised treatment to patients with bipolar disorder. To conduct scientific research for this new medicine, Zanopi would need access to large amounts of high-quality health data. Zanopi identifies several mental health clinics in the country of Nederlux (an EU Member State) that specialise in treating bipolar disorder and store their patients' data in Electronic Health Records (EHRs). Zanopi's researchers consider that electronic health data from EHRs would be very useful for research and development of their new medicine, since it includes structured information about health histories, diagnoses, as well as interventions and treatment outcomes.

1) Would Zanopi accessing and processing electronic health data from EHRs for drug research and development as imagined above qualify as secondary use of health data under the EHDS Regulation? Explain why yes/no. [2 pts]

2) Zanopi’s executives suggest to also use this EHR data for marketing purposes, to identify the individuals that might be good candidates for the new treatment once it reaches the market and advertise it to them. Does the EHDS Regulation allow this? Explain your answer. [1 pt]

3) Identify and explain the main reasons for regulating secondary use of health data in the EHDS Regulation, and in your answer, point out both the expected benefits and challenges that justified regulatory intervention. [2 pts]

4) The association representing patients with mental health problems in Nederlux, MINT, expresses concerns about sharing health data of patients with pharmaceutical companies in this way. Under the EHDS Regulation, do patients have any way to oppose their health data being shared for secondary use? Explain. Overall, do you consider the interests of citizens adequately recognised and upheld by the EHDS Regulation in cases of secondary use? Elaborate and justify your answer. [3 pts]

5) Mr. Barker is a patient in one of the clinics that Zanopi identified. He is planning to move from Nederlux to another EU Member State, where he would like to continue being treated for bipolar disorder. Will the doctor in Mr. Baker’s new country of residence be able to get access to information about Mr. Baker’s past medical test results and electronic prescriptions issued in the Nederlux clinic to (further) provide healthcare to Mr. Baker? Explain why yes/no. [2 pts]
