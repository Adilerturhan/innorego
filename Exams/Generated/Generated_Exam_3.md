## Question 1 — Module 1: Regulatory Strategies and Data Platform Regulation (25 points)

The European Union is considering how to regulate a new generation of health-monitoring wearable devices that continuously collect physiological data (heart rate, blood oxygen, sleep patterns) and use AI algorithms to provide health recommendations to users. These devices are manufactured by both EU-based and non-EU companies and are sold directly to consumers online.

The regulator is evaluating different regulatory approaches including:
- Command-and-control (prescriptive rules)
- Self-regulation and co-regulation
- Disclosure requirements
- Risk-based regulation
- Market-harnessing controls (competition-based)
- Design-based regulation (including nudging)

 a) Define and explain the core mechanisms of command-and-control, disclosure, and risk-based regulatory strategies. For each strategy, identify the key accountability levers and information requirements. (9 points)

Explain how the DSA combines disclosure and risk-based strategies. What are the strengths and limitations of this combined approach? (6 points)

 c) Discuss the concept of "regulatory disconnect" as presented by Brownsword. Identify and explain at least three variants of regulatory disconnect that could arise in regulating health-monitoring wearables. How does the "pacing problem" contribute to regulatory disconnect? (6 points)

 d) Propose a regulatory mix for the health-monitoring wearables described above. Your proposal should combine at least three different strategies. Justify your choices by explaining how each strategy addresses specific market failures or rights-based concerns, and discuss any trade-offs between the strategies you've chosen. (4 points)

## Question 2 — Module 2: EHDS Secondary Use and Data Permit Procedure (20 points)

MediResearch Institute, a private research organization registered in Germany with its principal place of business in Munich, requests access to health data for secondary use under the European Health Data Space (EHDS) Regulation.

- Electronic Health Records (patient summaries, lab results, and discharge reports) from hospitals in three Member States
- Data from wellness applications (fitness tracking, sleep monitoring) that users have integrated with their EHRs
- Regional cancer registry data
- Medical imaging data (X-rays and MRI scans)
- Administrative data on healthcare costs and insurance reimbursements

MediResearch proposes two research projects:

**Project A:** Develop an AI-based diagnostic support tool to assist radiologists in detecting early-stage lung cancer. The tool will be made available free of charge to public hospitals across the EU. MediResearch will retain the intellectual property rights and may license the technology to private healthcare providers for a fee.

**Project B:** Create a predictive model to identify individuals at high risk of developing chronic diseases, which will be sold as a subscription service to employers for workplace wellness programs and to insurance companies for risk assessment purposes.

 a) Applying Articles 51, 53, and 54 of the EHDS Regulation, assess whether each of the five data types listed above should be made available for secondary use. For each data type, explain your reasoning by reference to the permitted purposes, typical exclusions, and relevant safeguards under the EHDS. (8 points)

 b) Evaluate whether Project A constitutes a permitted purpose for secondary use under the EHDS. Identify any conditions or restrictions that the Health Data Access Body (HDAB) should impose on this project. (4 points)

 c) Evaluate whether Project B constitutes a permitted purpose for secondary use under the EHDS. Explain why this project would or would not be permitted, with reference to the prohibited purposes under Article 54. (4 points)

 d) State your final conclusion for each dataset and each project using "Yes/No/Uncertain" format, with a one-sentence justification for each. (4 points)

## Question 3 — Module 3: Competition Law Analysis (20 points)

HealthNet is a digital health platform operating across the EU with an 85% market share in the market for integrated healthcare appointment scheduling and telemedicine services. The platform has the following practices:

- **Exclusive dealing agreements:** HealthNet requires the top 10 private hospital chains in the EU to use only HealthNet's platform for all online appointment bookings and telemedicine consultations, in exchange for volume-based rebates of up to 30%.

- **Most-Favored Nation (MFN) clauses:** Contracts with healthcare providers stipulate that they must offer HealthNet pricing and terms that are no less favorable than those offered to any competing platform.

- **Bundling:** HealthNet requires hospitals that want to use its popular appointment scheduling module to also purchase its entire suite of services, including electronic health records management, billing software, and patient communication tools, even if hospitals already use specialized software for these functions.

- **Data practices:** HealthNet refuses to provide a new competitor, MediConnect, with access to anonymized patient flow data and appointment statistics that would allow MediConnect to optimize its scheduling algorithms. HealthNet argues that this data is its proprietary business information and providing it would undermine its competitive advantage.

 a) Define the relevant product market(s) and geographic market for HealthNet's services. Assess whether HealthNet holds a dominant position in the relevant market(s). Identify the key indicators and tests used to establish dominance under EU competition law. (5 points)

 b) Analyze the exclusive dealing agreements and MFN clauses under Article 101 TFEU. Your analysis should cover: (i) whether there is an agreement between undertakings; (ii) whether the agreement restricts competition by object or effect; (iii) whether the agreement appreciably affects trade between Member States; (iv) whether the agreement could qualify for an exemption under Article 101(3). (7 points)

 c) Analyze the bundling practice and refusal to provide data under Article 102 TFEU. Apply the relevant tests for abuse of dominance, including consideration of the separate products test for tying, foreclosure effects, and the essential facilities doctrine for refusal to supply. Discuss any objective justifications HealthNet might raise. (6 points)

 d) Conclude whether there are competition law infringements. State "Yes/No/Uncertain" and provide brief reasoning. If infringements are found, suggest proportionate remedies. (2 points)

## Question 4 — Module 4: Database Protection and Text and Data Mining (20 points)

WeatherPro is a Dutch company registered in the Netherlands with its registered office and principal place of business in Rotterdam. WeatherPro operates a comprehensive weather database that:

- Collects real-time weather data (temperature, humidity, wind speed, precipitation) from 5,000 weather stations across Europe at a cost of €8 million per year
- Verifies data accuracy using proprietary algorithms and a team of meteorologists, spending €3 million annually
- Organizes data by geographic coordinates (latitude/longitude), time stamps, and weather parameters using a standardized format common across the meteorological industry
- Provides a searchable interface allowing users to retrieve historical and current weather data for any location

WeatherPro makes its database available to research institutions under a license agreement that states: "Licensees may access and consult the database for research purposes only. Any form of automated extraction, data mining, or systematic downloading of database contents is strictly prohibited."

The University of Tilburg (TU) obtains a license for its Department of Environmental Sciences. Dr. Anna, a climate researcher employed full-time by TU, uses automated web scraping software to extract the entire weather dataset for the Netherlands covering the past 20 years. She needs this data to train a machine learning model that predicts extreme weather events for her publicly funded research on climate change adaptation. Her research is conducted solely for scientific purposes, and findings will be published in open-access journals. After training her model, Anna immediately deletes all the extracted weather data.

 a) Is WeatherPro's database a "database" within the meaning of Article 1(2) of the Database Directive? Apply all four criteria (collection, independence, systematic/methodical arrangement, individual accessibility) and explain whether each is satisfied. (4 points)

 b) Can WeatherPro's database qualify for copyright protection under Article 3 of the Database Directive? Apply the Author's Own Intellectual Creation (AOIC) standard of originality to the selection and/or arrangement of the database contents. Consider whether the standardized format and functional considerations prevent originality. (5 points)

 c) Can WeatherPro's database qualify for sui generis database right protection under Article 7 of the Database Directive? Assess: (i) whether the preliminary conditions of Article 11 are met; (ii) whether there has been substantial investment in obtaining, verification, or presentation; (iii) whether this is a "spin-off" database where investment was in creating rather than obtaining data. (5 points)

 d) Assuming the database is protected by both copyright and sui generis rights, can Dr. Anna rely on the exception in Article 3 of the DSM Directive for text and data mining for scientific research purposes? Apply the requirements: (i) Is TU a "research organization"? (ii) Does Dr. Anna have "lawful access"? (iii) Can the contractual prohibition override the Article 3 exception? Conclude with "Yes/No/Uncertain" and explain your reasoning. (6 points)

Identify appropriate safeguards SmartCity should implement. (4 points)

(4 points)
