### **Question 1: Regulatory Strategies & Innovation (Module 1) [20 Marks]**

A new technology, "Agri-Drone," is developed, allowing for the autonomous, large-scale spraying of highly effective pesticides. The technology promises to significantly increase crop yields and reduce labor costs for farmers. However, environmental groups are concerned that the drones could indiscriminately harm beneficial insect populations (like bees) and that pesticide runoff could contaminate water sources. The government is under pressure to act.

a) Describe how a "Command-and-Control" regulatory strategy could be applied to Agri-Drone technology. What are the potential drawbacks of this approach in the context of innovation? (8 marks)

b) Describe how a "Risk-Based Regulation" strategy could be applied. How would this differ from the command-and-control approach? (8 marks)

c) Argue which of the two strategies would be more appropriate for balancing the "regulation of innovation" (managing risks) with "regulation for innovativeness" (promoting benefits). (4 marks)

### **Question 2: European Health Data Space (Module 2) [20 Marks]**

A university research group, "Neuro-Innovate," is developing an AI model to predict the onset of neurodegenerative diseases. They apply to their national Health Data Access Body (HDAB) for secondary use of a dataset containing the following electronic health data from public health institutions:

1.  Data from medical devices, specifically brain-scan images (MRIs, fMRIs).
2.  Data that has been gathered for "official statistics" purposes, such as national disease prevalence reports.
3.  Data from clinical trials that were previously conducted to test a new Alzheimer's medication.

a) For each of the three data types requested, analyze whether it is a category of electronic health data that must be made available for secondary use under Article 51 of the EHDS proposal. Provide a justification for each. (9 marks)

b) Neuro-Innovate is a publicly funded university group, but it has a partnership with a private pharmaceutical company that will commercialize any resulting diagnostic tool. Is "scientific research" that leads to a commercial product a permitted purpose under Article 53? (5 marks)

c) An individual whose data is in the dataset had previously consented for their data to be used only for the original Alzheimer's clinical trial and no other purpose. Does the HDAB need to exclude this person's data from the set provided to Neuro-Innovate? (6 marks)

### **Question 3: Competition Law (Module 3) [20 Marks]**

"ConnectSphere" is a social media platform with a 90% market share in the EU. It faces a new, fast-growing rival, "PioneerNet," which offers innovative privacy-enhancing features. To neutralize this threat, ConnectSphere engages in two practices:

1.  It requires all major smartphone manufacturers that pre-install the ConnectSphere app to also sign an exclusivity clause, forbidding them from pre-installing the PioneerNet app.
2.  It identifies that PioneerNet's main appeal is its "secure messaging" feature. ConnectSphere rapidly develops a nearly identical feature and integrates it into its own platform, offering it for free. It then modifies its platform's technical protocols in a way that makes it difficult for users to share content from ConnectSphere to PioneerNet, citing "security reasons."

a) Analyze the exclusivity clause with smartphone manufacturers under Article 101 TFEU. Would this agreement be assessed by 'object' or 'effect'? Explain your reasoning. (10 marks)

b) Analyze ConnectSphere's actions (copying the feature and hindering interoperability) under Article 102 TFEU. What specific type of abuse could this conduct constitute, and what would a competition authority need to prove? (10 marks)

### **Question 4: Intellectual Property & Database Rights (Module 4) [20 Marks]**

A food blogger, Chloe, decides to create the ultimate guide to restaurants in her city. For one year, she visits hundreds of restaurants. For each one, she writes a short, creative review (2-3 sentences), gives it a unique "originality" score from 1-10, and records its name, address, and cuisine type. She then compiles all this information into a book, arranging the restaurants alphabetically. She also builds a website where users can search the same data.

A rival company, "FoodFind," systematically copies all the data from Chloe's website—the reviews, originality scores, names, addresses, and cuisine types—and uses it to populate its own competing app.

a) Is the collection of data on Chloe's website protected by **copyright** under the Database Directive? Justify your answer by applying the 'Author's Own Intellectual Creation' (AOIC) standard to the selection or arrangement of the contents. (8 marks)

b) Is the collection of data on Chloe's website protected by the ***sui generis* database right**? Justify your answer by analyzing whether Chloe has made a "substantial investment" in the obtaining, verification, or presentation of the data. (8 marks)

c) Assuming the *sui generis* right applies, has FoodFind's action constituted an infringement (i.e., 'extraction' or 're-utilization')? (4 marks)

### **Question 5: AI, Data Governance & Ethics (Module 5) [20 Marks]**

A city council wants to create an AI system to automatically grant or deny applications for small business permits. To build the training dataset, they use two sources:
1.  A public dataset of all business permits granted or denied in the city over the last 20 years. This data includes the business type, location, and outcome, but also the applicant's name and age.
2.  They perform web-scraping on a popular business networking site to gather data on the professional experience of past applicants, which is against the site's terms of service.

The council attempts to anonymize the dataset by removing the applicant names but keeps the age and business location data. The resulting AI model shows a strong tendency to deny permits to younger applicants and those in historically low-income areas.

a) Under the proposed EU AI Act, would this permit-granting system be classified as "high-risk"? Explain why. (5 marks)

b) Identify and explain two data governance requirements from Article 10 of the AI Act that the city council has likely violated in preparing their dataset. (8 marks)

c) The council claims the data is "anonymous" because they removed the names. Is this sufficient to be considered truly anonymized data? Explain the risk of re-identification in this context. (7 marks)
