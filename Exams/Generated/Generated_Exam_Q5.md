## Excerpts from Data-Driven Systems Accountability Act (DDSA)

### Section 5

Any organization seeking to deploy a data-driven system that significantly influences individuals' access to employment, housing, healthcare, or financial services must first obtain a Regulatory Certificate of Compliance from the Regulator.

1. The certificate shall be granted only after the system passes an independent technical audit demonstrating conformity with approved standards on fairness, transparency, and data integrity.
2. The Regulator may suspend or revoke certification where non-compliance or material risk is detected. It may also impose fines in cases of non-compliance caused by gross-negligence.

### Section 6

1. Prior to deployment, organizations shall conduct and publish an Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) describing:
   a. the system's purpose, scope, and decision logic;
   b. categories of personal and non-personal data used;
   c. foreseeable risks to individuals and mitigation measures adopted; and
   d. results of bias and error testing.
2. The AIA must be accessible to the public in a manner understandable to non-specialists.

### Section 8

1. The Regulator shall adopt a tiered supervisory framework under which oversight intensity corresponds to the assessed risk level of the system.
2. High-risk systems are subject to continuous monitoring and annual re-certification; moderate-risk systems every three years; and minimal-risk systems shall file a simplified declaration of compliance.
3. The Regulator may adjust risk tiers in light of emerging evidence or technological evolution.

### Section 9

1. Organisations must implement "ethics-by-design" principles, ensuring that risk mitigation, privacy protection, and fairness are integrated into the development process.
2. Voluntary participation in a Trusted Innovation Scheme grants certified organisations access to public procurement opportunities and regulatory fast-tracking.
