### **Question 1: Regulatory Strategies & Innovation (Module 1) [20 Marks]**

**a) Describe how a "Command-and-Control" regulatory strategy could be applied to Agri-Drone technology. What are the potential drawbacks of this approach in the context of innovation? (8 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 4 marks for description, 4 marks for drawbacks.
*   **Answer:**
    *   **Application:** A command-and-control strategy would involve setting strict, uniform, and legally binding rules. For Agri-Drones, this could include: a complete ban on their use within 5km of residential areas or water sources; a mandate that only specific, pre-approved types of pesticides can be used; and requirements for all operators to undergo a specific licensing procedure. Enforcement would be through inspections and penalties for non-compliance.
    *   **Drawbacks:** This approach is rigid and can stifle innovation. It locks in a specific technology or method, making it difficult for companies to introduce better, safer, or more efficient drone models or spraying techniques without a lengthy legislative change. It treats all contexts as the same, even though the risks may differ significantly between a large, isolated farm and one near a sensitive ecosystem. This can lead to over-regulation and discourage investment in improving the technology.

**b) Describe how a "Risk-Based Regulation" strategy could be applied. How would this differ from the command-and-control approach? (8 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 4 marks for description, 4 marks for the difference.
*   **Answer:**
    *   **Application:** A risk-based approach would focus regulatory resources on the highest-risk activities. Instead of a blanket ban, it would assess the specific risks of each use case. For example, using Agri-Drones in a dry, windy area near a bee farm would be classified as high-risk, requiring advanced GPS-targeting, specific nozzle types to reduce drift, and real-time monitoring. Use in a calm, isolated area might be low-risk, with fewer obligations. The regulation would set goals (e.g., "no harm to pollinators") rather than prescribing the exact method to achieve them.
    *   **Difference:** Unlike the one-size-fits-all rules of command-and-control, risk-based regulation is flexible and outcome-focused. It allows for innovation in compliance; companies can develop new technologies to meet the safety goals. It is more efficient, as it directs scrutiny where it is most needed, rather than imposing uniform burdens on everyone.

**c) Argue which of the two strategies would be more appropriate for balancing the "regulation of innovation" with "regulation for innovativeness." (4 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 4 marks for a clear argument.
*   **Answer:** **Risk-Based Regulation** is more appropriate. It effectively manages the risks ("regulation of innovation") by imposing strict controls on high-risk uses, protecting the environment and public health. Simultaneously, it promotes "regulation for innovativeness" by allowing low-risk applications to proceed with less friction and by giving companies the flexibility to innovate on safety and efficiency to meet regulatory goals. This targeted approach avoids unnecessarily stifling a beneficial technology, striking a much better balance than the rigid and potentially innovation-killing command-and-control strategy.

### **Question 2: European Health Data Space (Module 2) [20 Marks]**

**a) For each of the three data types requested, analyze whether it is a category of electronic health data that must be made available for secondary use under Article 51 of the EHDS proposal. (9 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 3 marks for each correct analysis.
*   **Answer:**
    1.  **Data from medical devices (brain-scans):** **Yes.** Article 51 explicitly lists "data from medical devices" as a priority category of electronic health data that must be made available for secondary use.
    2.  **Data from "official statistics":** **Yes.** Article 51 includes "health data from public registries for official statistics" as a required category for secondary use.
    3.  **Data from clinical trials:** **Yes.** The proposal includes "health data from clinical trials" as one of the categories that data holders are obliged to make available.

**b) Is "scientific research" that leads to a commercial product a permitted purpose under Article 53? (5 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 2 marks for "Yes," 3 marks for explanation.
*   **Answer:** **Yes.** Article 53 permits data access for "scientific research related to health" and for "development and innovation activities for products... that contribute to public health." The EHDS framework does not prohibit commercial outcomes, provided the primary purpose aligns with public health goals. Therefore, research by a university that leads to a commercialized diagnostic tool by a partner company is a permitted purpose.

**c) Does the HDAB need to exclude this person's data from the set provided to Neuro-Innovate? (6 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 2 marks for "Yes," 4 marks for explanation.
*   **Answer:** **Yes.** The EHDS proposal respects the principle of consent given under other frameworks like the GDPR and the Clinical Trials Regulation. If an individual provided specific, limited consent for their data to be used only in the original trial, that consent does not automatically extend to new secondary uses. The HDAB must respect the original scope of consent and exclude that individual's data from the dataset provided to Neuro-Innovate.

### **Question 3: Competition Law (Module 3) [20 Marks]**

**a) Analyze the exclusivity clause with smartphone manufacturers under Article 101 TFEU. Would this agreement be assessed by 'object' or 'effect'? (10 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 4 marks for identifying it as a vertical agreement. 6 marks for analyzing it as a restriction by 'effect'.
*   **Answer:**
    *   **Legal Issue:** The agreement between ConnectSphere (supplier of an app) and the smartphone manufacturers (distributors) is a **vertical agreement**. The clause is a form of single branding or exclusivity.
    *   **Analysis:** This agreement would be analyzed by its **'effect' on competition**. It is not a hardcore 'by object' restriction like price-fixing. The effect is to foreclose a competitor, PioneerNet, from an important channel of distribution. By preventing pre-installation on major smartphones, ConnectSphere makes it harder for PioneerNet to reach users, thus stifling its growth. This weakens competition in the social media market, protecting ConnectSphere's dominance and reducing consumer choice. The agreement has the clear effect of distorting competition.

**b) Analyze ConnectSphere's actions under Article 102 TFEU. What specific type of abuse could this conduct constitute? (10 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 3 marks for identifying dominance. 7 marks for identifying and explaining the abuse.
*   **Answer:**
    *   **Legal Issue:** This conduct is a potential abuse of a dominant position under Article 102 TFEU.
    *   **Analysis:**
        1.  **Dominant Position:** ConnectSphere has a 90% market share, which is a clear indication of dominance.
        2.  **Abuse:** The abuse is a form of **exclusionary conduct**. Specifically, it could be characterized as a "constructive refusal to supply" or a strategy of degrading interoperability. While dominant firms can compete, they have a special responsibility not to eliminate competitors through means other than competition on the merits. By copying the rival's key feature and then using its technical power to degrade interoperability, ConnectSphere is not competing on merits (e.g., by making a better product) but is leveraging its dominance in one area to foreclose a rival. The "security reasons" would likely be seen as a pretext. A competition authority would need to prove that this conduct is likely to foreclose an equally efficient competitor from the market, ultimately harming innovation and consumer choice.

### **Question 4: Intellectual Property & Database Rights (Module 4) [20 Marks]**

**a) Is the collection of data on Chloe's website protected by copyright? (8 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 2 marks for "Yes," 6 marks for applying the AOIC standard.
*   **Answer:** **Yes,** the database is likely protected by copyright, but only due to the creative elements included. While the *arrangement* (alphabetical) is standard and not original, the *selection* of contents includes Chloe's own intellectual creations: the short, creative reviews and the unique "originality" score. These elements are not mere facts; they are expressions of her personal judgment and creativity. Because the database's contents include these creative contributions, the selection of data is not purely factual and meets the "Author's Own Intellectual Creation" (AOIC) standard. The protection would cover these creative elements, not the raw facts (name, address).

**b) Is the collection of data on Chloe's website protected by the *sui generis* database right? (8 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 2 marks for "Yes," 6 marks for analyzing the investment.
*   **Answer:** **Yes,** it is highly likely to be protected by the *sui generis* right. This right protects a "substantial investment" in the **obtaining, verification, or presentation** of data. Chloe's year-long effort of visiting hundreds of restaurants, recording their details, and compiling them constitutes a substantial investment of time, effort, and financial resources in **obtaining** and **verifying** the contents of the database. This goes beyond simple data creation; it is an investment in collecting and organizing existing information into a new, valuable resource.

**c) Assuming the *sui generis* right applies, has FoodFind's action constituted an infringement? (4 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 4 marks for a clear "Yes" with justification.
*   **Answer:** **Yes.** The *sui generis* right prevents the "extraction" and/or "re-utilization" of the whole or a substantial part of the database. FoodFind systematically copied *all* the data from Chloe's website to populate its own app. This is a clear case of **extraction** (transferring the contents to another medium) and **re-utilization** (making the contents available to the public). This action directly undermines Chloe's investment and constitutes a clear infringement.

### **Question 5: AI, Data Governance & Ethics (Module 5) [20 Marks]**

**a) Under the proposed EU AI Act, would this permit-granting system be classified as "high-risk"? (5 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 2 marks for "Yes," 3 marks for reasoning.
*   **Answer:** **Yes.** Annex III of the AI Act lists as high-risk AI systems that are used for "access to and enjoyment of essential private services and public services and benefits." An AI system that automatically grants or denies business permits falls directly into this category, as it determines an individual's or company's access to a public service essential for their livelihood.

**b) Identify and explain two data governance requirements from Article 10 of the AI Act that the city council has likely violated. (8 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 4 marks for each violation.
*   **Answer:**
    1.  **Lawful and Ethical Data Collection:** The council used web-scraping against the terms of service of a website. This violates the principle that data should be collected in a lawful manner. Furthermore, it raises ethical concerns about using data for purposes for which it was not intended by the data subjects.
    2.  **Relevance, Representativeness, and Bias Examination:** Article 10 requires training data to be relevant, representative, and examined for biases. The dataset, based on 20 years of decisions, is contaminated with **historical bias**, reflecting past patterns of discrimination against younger people or certain areas. The council failed to adequately examine and mitigate this bias, leading to a discriminatory AI model, thus violating the requirement for data to be representative and free of bias.

**c) The council claims the data is "anonymous." Is this sufficient? Explain the risk of re-identification. (7 marks)**

*   **Marks:** 2 marks for "No," 5 marks for explaining re-identification risk.
*   **Answer:** **No,** simply removing names is not sufficient and is an example of pseudonymization, not anonymization. The risk of **re-identification** is very high. The dataset still contains quasi-identifiers like age and business location (which can be very specific, e.g., a postal code). By combining these data points, it would be relatively easy to identify an individual. For example, there may be only one 28-year-old who applied for a bakery permit in a specific neighborhood in a given year. By cross-referencing with other public information, the "anonymous" data can be linked back to a specific person, compromising their privacy. True anonymization requires techniques to prevent such re-identification.
