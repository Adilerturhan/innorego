# The GDPR

**Article 4 Definitions**

(1) 'personal data' means any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person ('data subject'); an identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, in particular by reference to an identifier such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier or to one or more factors specific to the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural or social identity of that natural person;

(2) 'processing' means any operation or set of operations which is performed on personal data or on sets of personal data, whether or not by automated means, such as collection, recording, organisation, structuring, storage, adaptation or alteration, retrieval, consultation, use, disclosure by transmission, dissemination or otherwise making available, alignment or combination, restriction, erasure or destruction;

*   Legal bases (Art. 6)
*   Principles of data processing (Art. 5)
*   Data subject rights (Ch. 3)
*   Obligations for controllers and processors (Ch. 4)

# The GDPR and health data

*   **Art. 4(15):** 'data concerning health' means personal data related to the physical or mental health of a natural person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about their health status (present, past, future) (see also Rec. 35)

In summary, personal data are health data when:

1.  The data are inherently/clearly medical data
2.  The data are raw sensor data that can be used in itself or in combination with other data to draw a conclusion about the actual health status or health risk of a person
3.  Conclusions are drawn about a person's health status or health risk (irrespective of whether these conclusions are accurate or inaccurate, legitimate or illegitimate, or otherwise adequate or inadequate)

**Article 9**
**Processing of special categories of personal data**

1. Processing of personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious or philosophical beliefs, or trade union membership, and the processing of genetic data, biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person, data concerning health or data concerning a natural person's sex life or sexual orientation shall be prohibited.
2. Paragraph 1 shall not apply if one of the following applies:

*   Higher level of protection for health data, but ...
*   'Scientific research regime' provides for flexibility
    *   Research exemption (Art. 9(2)(j)) in acc. with safeguards under Art. 89(1)
    *   Relaxation of purpose and storage limitation and derogations to data subject rights if their exercise would hamper research aims
*   States may maintain or introduce further conditions regarding health data processing (Art. 9(4))

# The DA

Regulation (EU) 2023/2854 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 December 2023 on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data and amending Regulation (EU) 2017/2394 and Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (Data Act) (Text with EEA relevance)
PE/49/2023/REV/1
OJ L, 2023/2854, 22.12.2023, ELI: http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/2854/oj (BG, ES, CS, DA, DE, ET, EL, EN, FR, GA, HR, IT, LV, LT, HU, MT, NL, PL, PT, RO, SK, SL, FI, SV)

*   Rules for B2C and B2B IoT data sharing
    *   **Art. 3:** Connected products and related services have to be designed in such a way that data is by default, easily, securely, comprehensively, free of charge, and in a structured, commonly used and machine-readable format directly accessible to the user
    *   **Art. 4:** data holders shall make readily available data, as well as the relevant metadata necessary to interpret and use those data, accessible to the user without undue delay
    *   **Art. 5:** data holders shall make readily available data, as well as the relevant metadata necessary to interpret and use those data, available to a third party upon request of the user, but ...
        *   Restrictions: no gatekeepers (Art. 5(3)) and not to develop competing products (Art. 6)
    *   Fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory arrangements (Art. 8) and compensation for making data available (Art. 9)
*   Rules for B2G data sharing based on exceptional need (Ch. 5)

# The EHDS

Regulation (EU) 2025/327 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 February 2025 on the European Health Data Space and amending Directive 2011/24/EU and Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 (Text with EEA relevance)
PE/76/2024/REV/1
OJ L, 2025/327, 5.3.2025, ELI: http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/327/oj (BG, ES, CS, DA, DE, ET, EL, EN, FR, GA, HR, IT, LV, LT, HU, MT, NL, PL, PT, RO, SK, SL, FI, SV)

[IMAGE: Flow chart diagram illustrating the goals and components of the EHDS, including electronic health records, health data from apps, health data in registries, and the resulting benefits.]

*   **Rationale:** to put forward a harmonised regime for secure and free movement of health data in the EU (see Rec. 110)
    *   Empower natural persons with control over their health data
    *   Foster internal makret for digital helath
    *   Ensure consistent and efficient framework for reuse of health data

**GROWTH POTENTIAL OF THE HEALTH DATA ECONOMY**

*   **€5.5 billion** (In savings in the EU over ten years from better access and exchange of health data in healthcare)
*   **20-30%** (Additional growth of the digital health market)
*   **€5.4 billion** (In savings in the EU over ten years from better use of health data for research, innovation and policy making)

*   The EHDSR thus establishes the common rules, rights, standards, infrastructures, and governance to facilitate access to health data (= personal or non-personal electronic health data (Rec. 6, Art. 2(2)(c))) for primary and secondary use (Art. 1)

# Primary use of health data

[IMAGE: Doctor interacting with a tablet displaying medical icons.]

*   Processing of health data for the provision of healthcare (Art. 2(2)(d))
*   Regulatory mechanisms:
    *   Priority categories of health data (Art. 14)
    *   European electronic health record exchange format (Art. 15) and interoperability and other requirements for EHRs (Ch. 3)
    *   Rights of natural persons (Art. 3-10) and the right of health professionals to access personal health data in EHRs (Art. 11)
        *   To facilitate the exercising of these rights, Member States should establish electronic health data access services (Art. 4) and health professional access services (Art. 12)
    *   Cross-border infrastructure MyHealth@EU (Art. 23)

---

# Secondary use of health data

[IMAGE: Stylized graphic showing data flowing from medical records on a tablet to microscopes, test tubes, and research analysis tools.]

*   Processing of health data for purposes other than initial purposes for which they were collected or produced (Art. 2(2)(e))
    *   Research, innovation, policy-making, education, patient safety, regulatory activities or personalised medicine
    *   Access to data for secondary use should contribute to the general interest of society (Rec. 61)
*   Regulatory mechanisms:
    *   Minimum categories of health data for secondary use (Art. 51)
        *   Data minimisation and purpose limitation (Art. 66)
    *   Delineation of allowed and prohibited purposes (Art. 53-4)
    *   Administrative procedure (a permit scheme) to facilitate secondary use (Art. 55)
    *   Infrastructure HealthData@EU (Art. 75)
