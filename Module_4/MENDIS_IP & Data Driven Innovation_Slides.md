# KC: Can data be protected under IP law?

**Data**
A collection of raw (unrefined), unorganized symbols (e.g. numbers or text) that do not carry specific meaning unless processed, organized and structured within a specific context.

**Q. Is data a product of human intellect?**
> No. Individual units of raw-data are not protected under IP law *as such* because they do not typically qualify as products of human intellect.

**Information**
Data that is processed, organized and structured within a specific context in order to enable analysis and the obtaining of knowledge.
> In certain situations – information & informational goods can be protected under IP rights by virtue of the human intellectual effort invested in their production.
*   Information: Trade secrets [EU Trade Secrets Directive (2016)].
*   Collections of data: Databases [EU Database Directive (1996)].
    *   Original databases: Copyright.
    *   Non-original databases: *Sui generis* database rights.

# Subject-matter

**What is a database?**
A collection of independent **data** arranged in a **systematic or methodical way** and **individually accessible** by electronic or other means. [Article 1(2) DD]

**Important:**
> Protection **does not** extend to the **individual units of data** contained in the database (but such content may on their own qualify for protection e.g. the individual photographs incorporated in a photography catalogue).

# Subject-matter

1.  **Collection:** A collection or compilation of data.
    > Data could be in the form of text, sound, images, numbers, facts etc.
2.  **Independence of data:** Data must constitute separate and independent items.
    > **Test:** Item(s) of data must have independent informative value when separated from database.
3.  **Systematic/methodical arrangement:** Data must be arranged in a systematic or methodical way (arrangement is logical not random).
4.  **Individual accessibility:** Independent units of data can be retrieved individually.
    > Collection should include technical or other means (e.g. electro-optical processes, an index or a particular plan or method of classification) to allow retrieval of independent items of data.

# Copyright protection

**Subject-matter**
Literary, artistic, scientific expression ("work") that is the product of human (?) intellect.
> A work is a creative combination of elements such as words, sounds, shapes or colors.

**Criteria**
Sole criterion: **Originality** (expression must be its Author's Own Intellectual Creation (**AOIC** standard of originality).

Work must reflect the author's **personal creative choices** leading to his **personal touch being stamped** on the expression.

1.  Creator has had the freedom to engage in his own (arbitrary) creative decision-making.
    *   AOIC standard will **not** be fulfilled when the creation process is dictated by **technical or functional considerations, rules or restrictions** thereby leaving no space for the exercise of free and creative choices.
2.  Creator has used this freedom to make personal creative choices in creating the expression. By reason of the creator making his personal creative choices the expression is stamped with the creator's personal touch.
    *   Mere expenditure of **skill, labour and know-how is insufficient** for fulling AOIC standard of originality!

# Scope

**Art. 5 DD:** Engaging in following acts (for commercial/ non-commercial purposes) require copyright owner's authorization:
a) Temporary/ permanent **reproduction (copying)** of database in whole or in part, by any means and in any form (directly/indirectly). [Does not cover mere consultation of a database made available to the public].
b) **Distribution (sale)** of database or its copies to the public.
c) **Adaptation or modification** of database (e.g. translation).
d) **Communication, display or performance** of the database to the public.

[**Communication:** transmission of the database by wire/wireless means including the making available to the public in such a way that members of the public may access them from a time and place individually chosen by them i.e., interactive on-demand transmissions].
> Communication should be made to a “**new public**", i.e., a public to whom the rightholder did not intend to make work available. E.g., A made copyright protected database available only to licensed subscribers. B copied the database without A's permission and posted it on his personal website, thereby making it available also to those who had not obtained a license. These persons constitute a “new public”.

# Preliminary condition: Who can benefit from protection?

**Article 11 DD:** *Sui generis* right can only be obtained if the **maker of the database is**:
> Natural person: Who holds nationality of an EU Member State or maintains habitual residence in the territory of the EU.

> Legal person: A company or firm that fulfills **both** of following criteria.
1.  Formed in accordance with the law of an EU Member State and having its registered office within the community.

2.  Having its central administration **or** principal place of business within the Community. [Because operations of company must be genuinely linked on an ongoing basis with the economy of an EU Member State].

# Criteria

**Art. 7 DD:** There must have been a **substantial investment** made in either the **obtaining, verification or presentation** of contents of database.

> **Obtaining:** investment made in seeking out and collecting existing data (even if the initial objective of collecting the data was not for creating the database per se.

> **Verification:** investment made in ensuring the accuracy/reliability of the data during collection and afterwards (periodic monitoring the 'materials' of a database to ensure completeness and accuracy ).

> **Presentation:** Selection and/or arrangement of contents in a way that is individually accessible.

**Investment:** Can be financial, human effort, expertise, time, know-how, skill and labour etc.

# Difference between creation and obtaining of data

Investment made in **creating (generating) data or verifying data at the point of creation is not relevant** in determining whether a substantial investment has been made in obtaining or verifying contents of database!

“Spin-off” databases (i.e., compilations of data that are generated as by-products ('spin-offs') of other activities cannot claim *sui generis* database protection on the basis of having made an investment in the obtaining (i.e., collection) of data, because the investment is not made in obtaining existing data but in engaging in a different activity as a result of which the data is generated.

E.g., A TV program schedule that is generated by a TV station, a database of flight schedules generated by an airline company.
[more information, https://www.ivir.nl/publicaties/download/spinofffordham.pdf]

# Scope

**Art. 7:** Engaging in following acts (for commercial/ non-commercial purposes) require *sui generis* right owner's authorization:

*   **Extraction** and/or **re-utilization** of whole or substantial part of contents of database evaluated qualitatively and/ or quantitatively.
    > Includes, repeated and systematic extraction and/or re-utilization of insubstantial parts of the contents of database where this is contrary to the normal exploitation of that database or unreasonably prejudices the legitimate interests of the producer of the database. [Art. 7(5) DD].

**How to test "Substantiality**
> **Quantitative:** Volume of data assessed in relation to total volume of contents of database.
> **Qualitative:** Scale of investment in obtaining, verification and presentation of contents extracted/re-utilized is substantial.

# Substantial investment?

Difficult to distinguish between ‘creating' and 'obtaining' when big data is generated, aggregated, processed and exchanged in real-time.

*   Gervais: Big Data corpora are neither “obtained” nor “collected"; they are generated by the machine. [Gervais, 2019, 29].
*   But, sometimes machine-generated databases generate data based on observed occurrences.
    > Data from an automatically administered database can be deemed as 'obtained' when it records a physical or digital, objectively appreciable occurrence (i.e., data observed and recorded by a satellite).

# What is lawful access?

When the database is accessed via a **lawful source** [CJEU, C 435/12, *ACI Adam* [2014]; C-463/12 *Copydan* [2015]]
> Access of database is with **rightholders' consent**.

E.g. Accessing a database made available under an **open access license** or by an **authorized person under a contract that permits such access** (Recital 14 of DSM Directive).

If you have accessed content which has been made available **without rightholders' consent** (via an unlawful source), user must **not have known nor had reasonable grounds to know** that it was made available without rightholder's consent.
> But, such knowledge is presumed in the case of use for 'profit-making activities'.

# What is lawful use? Who is a lawful user?

Cumulative criteria for assessing "lawful use".

1.  **Access is via a lawful source** - The content is made available to the public with the rightholder's consent.
    > Lawful access is a precondition for lawful use, even if the use is covered by an exception or limitation [CJEU, C 435/12, *ACI Adam* [2014]].

2.  **Specific use is authorized by the rightholder** (e.g., contractual terms OR open-access license authorizing use).
    OR;
    Lack of warnings, or contractual restrictions should not be taken as implicit authorization.
    **Specific use is not prevented by law.** E.g.,
    > There are no IP rights, contractual terms that prevent use;
    > Specific use is permitted under a legally granted exception that cannot be overridden by contract).

# CJEU, C-30/14, *Ryanair Ltd v PR Aviation BV* [2015]

CJEU said **NO!**

*   Ryanair's database does **NOT** benefit from protection either under copyright OR the *sui generis* right.
*   Article 6(1) exception is therefore **not applicable**.
    > If a database is not protected under the Database Directive (either copyright or *sui generis* right) the owner has the freedom to **adopt of contractual clauses** concerning the conditions of use of such a database.

So, **exceptions and limitations or rights of lawful users** that apply to databases protected under the Database Directive (including datamining exceptions under CDSM) **do not apply to unprotected databases!**
