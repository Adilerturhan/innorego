### Question 2
0 / 1 pts

The investment made in the creation of data and verifying the accuracy of that data at the point of creation is relevant for determining whether a database can obtain protection under the **sui generis** database right.

*   True
*   **False**

**Wrong answer: True**

**Correct answer: False** (The *sui generis* right (Article 7 DD) protects investment in the **obtaining, verification or presentation** of the contents, but specifically **excludes** investment made in the *creation* of the data itself. The phrase "at the point of creation" makes the statement false, as per the established case law (e.g., *British Horseracing Board*).)

### Question 3
0 / 1 pts

In order to benefit from the data-mining exception provided in **Article 4** of the **Copyright in the Digital Single Market Directive (2019)** it is necessary that a user must have lawful access to a database through a research organization or cultural heritage institution (as defined in Article 2 of that Directive).

*   True
*   **False**

**Correct answer: False** (The exception provided in Article 4 CDSM (general text and data mining exception) applies to any user with lawful access. The narrower exception for scientific research, which is restricted to research organizations and cultural heritage institutions, is found in **Article 3** CDSM.)

### Question 4
1 / 1 pts

The data-mining exception provided in **Article 3** of the **Copyright in the Digital Single Market Directive (2019)** can **not** be overriden by contract.

*   **True**
*   False

**Correct answer: True** (Article 7(1) CDSM provides that any contractual provision contrary to the exceptions laid down in Articles 3 and 4 shall be unenforceable. Thus, the Article 3 exception cannot be overridden by contract.)

### Question 5
0 / 1 pts

Even when a database is not protected either by copyright or the sui generis database right, users of the database are still able to rely on the exceptions for data-mining provided in Articles 3 and 4 of the Copyright in the Digital Single Market Directive (2019) .

*   **False**
*   True

**Correct answer: False** (The exceptions in Articles 3 and 4 CDSM relate to **copyright** protection and apply to databases protected by copyright. They do **not** apply to the *sui generis* database right. For a database protected only by the *sui generis* right (Article 7 DD), contractual opt-outs are generally permitted, and there is no mandatory exception for text and data mining. Therefore, users cannot rely on CDSM exceptions against an unprotected database or one protected only by the *sui generis* right.)

### Question 8

Assuming that the database was protected under the **sui generis** database right, would Jan's use of web-crawlers to identify and evaluate correlations in the data constitute an act that falls within the scope of DataMed's sui generis database right?

*   **Yes. Because it constitutes an extraction of the whole or a substantial part of the database.**
*   Yes. Because it constitutes a re-utilization of the whole or a substantial part of the database.
*   Yes. Because it constitutes a distribution of the contents of the database.

**Correct answer: Yes. Because it constitutes an extraction of the whole or a substantial part of the database.** (The *sui generis* right (Article 7 DD) grants the rightsholder the exclusive right to prevent extraction (transferring contents to another medium) and re-utilization (making contents available to the public). Training an AI algorithm inherently involves extraction and copying, which is prevented by the *sui generis* right.)
