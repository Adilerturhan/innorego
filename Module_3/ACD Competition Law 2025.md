## Case 1: Commission v Microsoft

The majority of the computers used by businesses/corporate offices in the EU are compatible with Microsoft and therefore the majority of the corporate offices in the EU purchase work computers with pre-installed Microsoft. Seeing this advantage, Microsoft introduces a new version of Windows specifically designed for the corporate offices that only fully supports its own Office software while not allowing the performance and compatibility software of competitors such as Google Docs, and Libre Office. Google reports this to the Commission and the Commission initiates an investigation into a possible infringement of EU competition law.

**Analyze whether Microsoft's actions constitute an abuse of dominance under EU competition law principles, considering the potential anticompetitive effects and the possible justifications Microsoft might offer.**

## Case 1: Commission v Microsoft

- **Undertaking** - check
- **Dominant position** - check
- **Abuse**
- **Whether it affects the EU market(s) or a substantial part of it** – **even one member state suffices.**

## Case 1: Commission v Microsoft

1) To establish an abuse of a dominant position, first, we need to establish whether it is done by an **undertaking** An undertaking is any economic entity that engages in commercial activity. Microsoft is an economic entity that engages in commercial activities and therefore is an undertaking.
2) Second, we need to see whether the undertaking is in dominant position. A dominant position is given when a competitor can act independently on the market. Here, Microsoft can exclude other service providers in the relevant product market in an independent way. Therefore, Microsoft could have a dominant position. Let's assume that it does.
3) Third, an abuse must have taken place. There is no single definition for an abuse but the behaviour at hand that an abuse is exclusion of competitors from the performance and compatibility software (we can call them office'-related software) market. An abuse has taken place.
4) The market must also be distorted in a substantial part of the internal market, which is defined as already taking place when one Member State is affected sufficiently.
5) No justification of the action may be applicable. An anti-competitive behaviour may be justified if the gains for the consumer outweigh the harm for the market. Here, Libra Office or Google docs users cannot use these in their office computers. Hence, the gains are not translated to the consumer. The action is not justified.

**Concluding, the action at hand is an abuse of a dominant position and therefore a violation of Article 102 TFEU.**
