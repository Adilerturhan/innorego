# Merger Control - Regulation (EC) No 139/2004

## Merger Control

**Article 2(3) EUMR:**

3. A **concentration** which would **significantly impede effective competition**, in the **common market** or in a **substantial part** of it, in particular as a result of the creation or strengthening of a dominant position, shall be declared incompatible with the common market.

## Legal Test (simplified)

1) Concentration
2) EU Dimension
3) Significantly Impede Effective Competition
4) In a substantial part of the internal market
5) Prior Notification

---

# Merger Control - Regulation (EC) No 139/2004

## 1) Concentration

a) **Merger:** Two previously independent undertakings become one (e.g. 14 directors, 7 from each side)
b) **Acquisition:** One undertaking gains control over another (e.g. holding > 50% shares)

## 2) EU Dimension: A certain threshold must be met of turnover (e.g. a combined worldwide turnover of > 5000 mio. Euro and a EU-wide turnover of > 250 mio. Euro)

## 3) Significantly impede effective competition

a) **Through non-coordinated effects:** risk of significant price increase through loss of competition
b) **Through coordinated effects:** coordinating behaviour outside of Article 101 TFEU

## 4) In a substantial part of the internal market:** Dominance in already one EU Member State is sufficient

## 5) Prior Notification:** European Commission must be notified prior to merger/acquisition

# Anti-Competitive Behaviour – Article 101 TFEU

**'Cartel-Prohibition'**

### Article 101
(ex Article 81 TEC)

1. The following shall be prohibited as incompatible with the internal market: all **agreements** between **undertakings**, **decisions by associations of undertakings** and **concerted practices** which may **affect trade between Member States** and which have as their **object or effect** the **prevention, restriction or distortion of competition** within the internal market, and in particular those which:

(a) directly or indirectly fix purchase or selling prices or any other trading conditions;
(b) limit or control production, markets, technical development, or investment;
(c) share markets or sources of supply;
(d) apply dissimilar conditions to equivalent transactions with other trading parties, thereby placing them at a competitive disadvantage;
(e) make the conclusion of contracts subject to acceptance by the other parties of supplementary obligations which, by their nature or according to commercial usage, have no connection with the subject of such contracts.

## Legal Test (simplified)

1) Between Undertakings
2) Agreements or concerted practices
3) Effect on trade between Member States
4) Effect on competition by object or effect
5) No justification according to Art. 101(3) TFEU

---

# Anti-Competitive Behaviour – Article 101 TFEU

**1) Between Undertakings:** Any entity that engages in commercial/economic activities, whether or not such activities are carried out for profit (e.g. *Microsoft*)

**2) Agreements or concerted practices**

**3) Effect on trade between Member States:** If the pattern of trade between at least two Member States is impacted – this may be direct/indirect, actual/potential ≠ not if effect is insignificant

**4) Effect on competition by object or effect**

a) **By object** – 'hard core cartels' (e.g. *price fixing*)

b) **By effect** – when it can be foreseen that an agreement/concerted practice has a negative effect on prices, output, innovation, variety of goods/services or quality of goods/service ≠ not if effect is insignificant

---

# What is an agreement under competition law?

a) **Agreement:** Any agreement – written/oral, no physical meeting necessary, phone call/email may suffice = parties determine how they behave on the market (e.g. *price fixing*)

i. **Horizontal agreements:** Between two competitors operating at the same level of the supply chain (e.g. *Spotify & Apple Music; price fixing*)

ii. **Vertical agreements:** Between undertakings at different levels of the supply chain (e.g. *manufacturer – distributor; fixing retail price*)

b) **Concerted Practice:** Informal cooperation between undertakings without any formal agreement or decision = parties reveal their strategies (e.g. *information exchange*)

"The competitors know each other so well, even a wink and a nod between them could lead to an anti-competitive behaviour."

# Abuse of Dominant Position – Article 102 TFEU

## Abuse of Dominance

### Article 102
(ex Article 82 TEC)

Any **abuse** by one or more **undertakings of a dominant position** within the **internal market or in a substantial part of it** shall be prohibited as incompatible with the internal market in so far as it may **affect trade between Member States**.

Such abuse may, in particular, consist in:

(a) directly or indirectly imposing unfair purchase or selling prices or other unfair trading conditions;
(b) limiting production, markets or technical development to the prejudice of consumers;
(c) applying dissimilar conditions to equivalent transactions with other trading parties, thereby placing them at a competitive disadvantage;
(d) making the conclusion of contracts subject to acceptance by the other parties of supplementary obligations which, by their nature or according to commercial usage, have no connection with the subject of such contracts.

## Legal Test (simplified)

1) Abuse
2) Undertaking
3) Dominant position
4) In a substantial part of the internal market
5) No objective justification

---

# Abuse of Dominant Position – Article 102 TFEU

## Abuse of Dominance (Cont.)

**1) Abuse:** No single definition but abusive behaviour examples included in Art 102(a)-(d) TFEU (non-exhaustive)

**2) Undertaking(s):** Any entity that engages in commercial/economic activities, whether or not such activities are carried out for profit (e.g. *Microsoft*)

**3) Dominant Position:** A position of economic strength which enables the undertaking to prevent effective competition by giving it the power to behave to an appreciable extent independently of its competitors and consumers

**4) In a substantial part of the internal market:** Dominance in already one EU Member State is sufficient

**5) No objective justification**

*\*A dominant position in itself is not an infringement of Article 102 TFEU – only the abuse of its dominance to reduce/distort competition in the market. This gives a special responsibility to a dominant company. It also means that what is abusive for a dominant company may not be illegal for a non-dominant company.*

# Microsoft Case

## Setting the State for Competition Law & Data: *Microsoft* – Case COMP/C-3/37.39

One of the first cases to raise the question of access to data in competition law

*   Microsoft, **dominant company** for computer operating system, withheld interface information data from competitors in the secondary software market
*   Competitors who wanted to offer software, which is **interoperable with Windows operating system**, could **not enter the market** due to lack of data
*   European Commission held that this is an **abuse of a dominant position** = infringement of EU competition law
*   Decision was upheld at General Court (CJEU)
*   Microsoft was fined almost 500 mio. Euros + was ordered to grant licenses on reasonable & non-discriminatory terms

# German META Case

*   Bundeskartellamt prohibited Meta from combining user data from several sources without the users' consent.
*   Meta appealed this decision to the Higher Regional Court of Düsseldorf.
*   The Düsseldorf court asked the CJEU to provide clarification on how to interpret certain provisions of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and whether the Bundeskartellamt may also take into consideration GDPR norms when weighing interests in decisions under competition law.
*   CJEU → Data protection considerations **CAN BE TAKEN INTO ACCOUNT** in an abuse of dominance case by also ensuring cooperation with DPAs.

# Competition Law: EU vs US

| Category | EU | US |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Objective** | Internal Market (although it is increasingly becoming more about consumer welfare) | Consumer Welfare – strictly economic approach |
| **Competition Law “Tools”** | 1. Anti-competitive behavior<br>2. Abuse of dominant position<br>3. Merger control<br>4. State aid | 1. Anti-competitive behavior<br>2. Abuse of dominant position<br>3. Merger control |
| **Enforcement** | By Administrative system | By criminal law |
| **Enforcement actors** | European Commission<br>National competition authorities | Department of Justice<br>Federal Trade Commission |
| **Fines** | Max. 10% of the annual overall turnover (worldwide) | Up to 100 mio. US Dollars or higher fines based on profits and damages incurred |

# Cooperation between EU & US

*   European Commission and US authorities (DOJ & FTC) **cooperate** on cases that affect both jurisdictions
*   EU can **request** US to take action when it sees its own market adversely affected by an action that is taking place in the US territory (and vice versa)
*   But either jurisdiction will only look at their **own markets**, which means they can come to **different results** regarding the same case (e.g. merger control)
*   Cooperation may be based on Cooperation Agreement (1991), Positive Comity Agreement (1998), Best Practices Document (2011)

# US Microsoft Case

## Example of US Case

**Abuse of Dominance: *United States v Microsoft***

*   The U.S. government accused Microsoft of abusing its dominant position in the web browser market for Windows
*   The abusive behaviour was primarily through the legal and technical restrictions it put on the abilities of PC manufacturers (OEMs) and users to uninstall Internet Explorer and use other programs such as Netscape and Java.
*   At the initial trial, the United States District Court for the District of Columbia ruled that Microsoft's actions constituted unlawful monopolization under Section 2 of the Sherman Antitrust Act
*   U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit partially overturned that judgment
*   The two parties later reached a settlement in which Microsoft agreed to modify some of its business practices.
